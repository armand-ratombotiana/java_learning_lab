# Company Behavioral Guide

## The STAR Method

Every behavioral answer follows this structure:

| Component | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **S**ituation | Context — what was the background? | "Our team was responsible for deploying a recommendation model serving 10M users." |
| **T**ask | Your specific responsibility | "I needed to reduce inference latency from 200ms to under 50ms to meet a new SLA." |
| **A**ction | What *you* specifically did (not the team) | "I implemented KV-cache optimization and switched to FP16 quantization, reducing model size by 50%." |
| **R**esult | Measurable outcome | "Latency dropped to 45ms p99, saving $200K/month in GPU costs." |

## Common Behavioral Questions by Category

### Leadership & Initiative
- "Tell me about a time you led a technical project."
- "Describe a situation where you influenced a team decision."
- "Tell me about a time you mentored a junior engineer."

### Conflict & Collaboration
- "Describe a disagreement with a teammate and how you resolved it."
- "Tell me about a time you worked with a difficult stakeholder."
- "How do you handle competing priorities from different teams?"

### Failure & Learning
- "Tell me about a time something went wrong in production."
- "Describe a project that failed and what you learned."
- "Tell me about a technical decision you later regretted."

### Technical Leadership
- "Describe a system you designed from scratch."
- "Tell me about the most complex technical problem you solved."
- "How do you approach technical debt while shipping features?"

### AI-Specific Behavioral Questions
- "Tell me about a time you improved model quality through prompt engineering."
- "Describe a production AI incident you debugged."
- "How do you balance model accuracy with inference cost?"
- "Tell me about a time you detected and mitigated bias in a model."
- "Describe your approach to A/B testing a new prompt template."

## FAANG-Specific Themes

### Amazon Leadership Principles
| Principle | How to Address |
|-----------|----------------|
| Customer Obsession | Start every answer with customer impact |
| Ownership | Take responsibility for outcomes, not just tasks |
| Invent and Simplify | Explain how you simplified a complex system |
| Are Right, A Lot | Use data to justify decisions |
| Learn and Be Curious | Show you explored multiple solutions |
| Insist on the Highest Standards | Describe your testing/QA process |
| Think Big | Connect your work to long-term vision |
| Bias for Action | Show you shipped quickly when appropriate |
| Dive Deep | Demonstrate understanding of low-level details |
| Deliver Results | Always end with measurable outcomes |

### Google Googleyness
- **Comfort with ambiguity**: Take initiative in undefined spaces
- **Collaboration**: Put team success above personal credit
- **Intellectual humility**: Willingness to be wrong, learn from others
- **Data-driven**: Back opinions with metrics and experiments

### Meta's Move Fast
- **Speed**: Show how you shipped quickly without sacrificing quality
- **Impact**: Quantify the business/user impact of your work
- **Iteration**: Demonstrate your ability to launch → learn → improve

## Answering Framework

1. **Listen fully** — Don't interrupt, ask clarifying questions
2. **Choose the right story** — Pick a story that matches the question
3. **Set the context (ST)** — 1-2 sentences for situation and task
4. **Describe your action (A)** — 3-4 sentences on what YOU did
5. **Share the result (R)** — Quantify with metrics, include learning
6. **Keep it concise** — 2-3 minutes max per answer

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Taking all credit** — Use "we" appropriately but highlight "I"
- **Vague results** — Never skip the result; use numbers
- **Blaming others** — Own your failures, show what you learned
- **Too much jargon** — Interviewers may not know your domain
- **Ignoring the question** — Answer directly before adding context
